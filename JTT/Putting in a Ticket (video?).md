Tickets can be confusing. When should I put it in, where does it go, could this just be an email, etc.

I'm hoping that after this Tech Tip, they will be a little less daunting and help to speed up your service.

Too Lond, Didn’t Read:

                Tickets are important for documentation of issues and speed of resolution. A ticket stays around while a text or email gets lost in all the other noise. If you ever ask yourself “should I put a ticket in for this or just email Jeremy?” The answer is “put in the ticket”. You can even do both, CC me on the email sent to TDX.

**Skip to the end if you need a refresher on how to put in a ticket**
# When to put in a ticket
If you've ever asked yourself "Do I need to put in a ticket for this?", 97% of the time the answer is "YES". Some examples are:
- "Can you put this app on my iPad(s)?" - Yes
- "Is this something that you can fix?" - probably
- "I need a piece of specific hardware (monitor, Apple Pencil, etc.) can you get it for me?" - I can't purchase it, but we can work with the Head Admin Assistant to get something purchased and then i can set it up - **I need a ticket to track the project**
- "I or my student can't login to a specific app" - more likely the Help Desk, but I need a ticket to send to them.

# Doesn't need a ticket
I always encourage you to put in a ticket. I can always close a ticket after talking with you about where to go. These are things that don't need a ticket to me:
- **custodial** - still needs a ticket, just not to me.
- **How to use instructional software** (Wonders, 95%, etc) - Your Coach is the best resource for how to use software, there's only a few I can help you with:
	- Lanschool
	- Microsoft word, Outlook
	- screen mirroring
	- using your browser


# Why does it matter?
I want to make sure that you get the help you need as FAST AS POSSIBLE. **I check my tickets first thing in the morning** and I try to keep an eye on emails, but tickets are my priority. Tickets are nice because they don't go away until I mark them resolved. Emails stack up and get lost. I'm also not good at remembering stuff without some kind of reminder and an email is not a good reminder because it gets buried. 

Tickets also act as a documentation form. They show the history of what we saw and updated; it can be a good way to pull data related to a specific issue. If you have 5 tickets all saying that your email stops working on St. Patricks Day, we can go back to all of them and see the pattern. Emails don't give us that granularity because they are lost.

**Speed**. Tickets, when put in correctly, make it so much easier to re-assign or resolve an issue is a quick fashion. We have all the detail and sometimes it even auto-assigns to the right department, depending on the issue.
