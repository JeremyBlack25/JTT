# What printers do I have?
Hey everyone! Welcome to the first video of what i'm calling the "Bite-sized Printer TIps". These are short videos that are max of 2 minutes that are meant to "de-mistify" printers. 

I think we can all agree that printers are weird and confusing, hopefully after this series you can feel a little more confident when approaching a printer.

Jumping right in...

Most schools have multiple printers that you can print to and sometimes we only use one, but what if that one is broken and you desperately need to print but you don't know what printers you have, let alone how to actually select it!

No stress, i'm going to show you 2 ways to see what printers you have and how to select them to print to.

I'll have a side by side view for the different views depending what kind of a computer you have. (Monterey on the left, Sequioa on the right.)

1. first thing to do is open your settings.
2. then find your printers, should be at the bottom, or in the middle if you have a newer version.
3. Then you should see all the printers that are connected to your computer.

Second way to see, and how to change what one you are using

1. Open a document
	1. I have this document for testing purposes
2. Once you have a document you want, there are 2 ways to print
	1. File > Print
	2. cmd + P
3. Here is where you can see what printer is currently selected and will be used if you press print.
4. Now, say this printer isn't working or you want to print to a black and white one, or another color one.
5. All you need to do is select your current printer and you see a list of the printers connected. 

We have standard names for each printer to make it easy to know where you're sending your print job to. If you can't figure out what you have, feel free to reach out to your field tech to get them straightened out.

# What are printer drivers

Welcome to another "Bite-sized Printer Tips". An attempt to de-mistify printers.

You'll hear techs talk about printer drivers, and you may wonder what in the world those could be. Basically, just about anything that connects to a computer needs drivers, not just printers.

When you send a print to a printer it goes through the driver first. The driver then translates the document into a version that the printer can understand. Like translators helping you understand a conversation for a language you don't know.

With most devices; USB flashdrives, mice, keyboards, etc, the computer already has generic drivers to use and you don't have to install anything. There are generic drivers for printers too, but they don't work as well, especially as you get more complex things to print. So it makes installing specific printers drivers even more vital.

# Cost of Printing

Welcome to the ***last*** "Bite-sized Printer Tips". An attempt to de-mistify printers.

Something I found super surprising while doing research on printers is how much it can actually cost to print something. Like it adds up fast type of printing.

According to a few sources I found, it can cost anywhere from 20 to 25 cents a page for color printing! I mean, that's insane! Black and White printing is closer to 20 cents per page.

Now this is your typical office printer, copiers are cheaper, 10 to 15 cents per page. For Black and White, and just slightly more for color.

The moral of the story i guess is to be conceise of how much you are printing, and if you can choose to print on a copier, that's a great option. Or print to your printer and make copies at your closest copier.
