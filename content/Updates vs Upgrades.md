## Updates vs Upgrades
**Google Gemini Says:** "An update is a minor change to your current software or system that fixes bugs, patches security flaws, or adds small features while keeping you on the same version.
An upgrade is a major change that replaces your software or hardware with a brand-new version, introducing significant new features and structural changes."

These two terms are used interchangeably a lot of the time, so don't be stressed if you don't "use it right". 

## Why do I bring this up?
Apple has just released a new MacOS version. this is a major upgrade from the last version. The new version is called MacOS 27 or Golden Gate. for the last 10ish years, Apple has named all there OSs after locations in California, if that helps you know what their reasoning is.

With this new upgrade coming out, I wanted to help you understand what the District's expectations are when it comes to updating/upgrading.
1. We fully encourage all updates (eg. 26.7.1 -> 26.8.2) including applications.
	1. This helps to keep us secure becuase Apple Still supports older OSs.
	2. Apple still supports MacOS 15 and 26 (yeah, they skipped a few models)
2. For now we want to hold off on upgrading to MacOS 27 Golden Gate. 

## Apple's Newest MacOS
Apple has a tendency to break things with their new OSs, and we usually wait until December or January before we push the upgrades. This gives Apple enough time to work out all the kinks so we don't have any broken or weird computers and you can all keep teaching!
