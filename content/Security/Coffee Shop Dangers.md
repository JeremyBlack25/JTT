## Coffee Shop Dangers: Think Before You Connect

We’ve all been there—you’re at your favorite local coffee shop, you’ve got your drink, and you want to get a little bit of grading or lesson planning done. You open your laptop, see "Free Shop WiFi" with no password required, and click connect.

It feels convenient, but connecting to unsecured or unfamiliar networks can be like leaving your classroom door wide open with a sign that says "Everyone Welcome—Help Yourself!"

#### Too Long Didn't Read: TL;DR

Unsecured WiFi (networks without passwords) allows hackers to "listen in" on what your computer is doing. **How to stay safe:** * Avoid "Public" or "Open" networks for sensitive work.

- Ask an employee for the wifi information.
- Use your phone's **Hotspot** if you need to access school accounts.
- Look for the **Padlock icon** in your browser address bar.
- Forget the Wifi from your settings when you're done so it won't auto-connect again.

---

### What’s the big deal?

When you connect to a WiFi network that doesn't require a password, the data leaving your computer isn't "scrambled" (encrypted). This means anyone else on that same network with the right (and easily available) tools can see what you are doing.

Imagine you are whispering a secret to a friend in a crowded room. On a secure network, it's like whispering in a code only you two know. On an unsecured network, it's like shouting that secret through a megaphone.

### The "Evil Twin" Attack

Sometimes, hackers set up a fake WiFi network with a name you trust, like **"Starbucks_Guest_FREE"** or **"Library_WiFi."** If you connect to their "Evil Twin" network instead of the real one, they act as a middleman, watching every password you type and every email you send.

### How to Protect Your Data (And Our Students!)

As teachers, we handle sensitive student data and district information. Here is how to keep it safe when you're out and about:

1. **Use Your Personal Hotspot:** Your phone’s hotspot is a private, password-protected connection. It is much safer than any public WiFi.
2. **Verify the Network:** If you must use the shop's WiFi, ask the barista for the exact name of their network and the password. If it doesn't have a password, try to avoid using it for anything important (like bank accounts or @csddocs.org).
3. **Check for the Padlock:** Look at the web address in your browser (Chrome or Firefox). If you see a little padlock icon next to the URL, your connection to that specific website is encrypted. If it says "Not Secure," get out of there!
4. **Log Out:** When you're finished, "Forget" the network in your settings so your computer doesn't automatically reconnect the next time you walk by.
