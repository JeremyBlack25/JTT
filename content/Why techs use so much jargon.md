I think it's safe to assume we've all been there. You're talking with the cashier at Best Buy, or with your grandkids shootin' the breeze, or with someone that's from a different generation talking up a storm. Suddenly they say a word or phrase that you've never heard before:

**For Gen Z that could be:** "Rizz", "no cap", "bet", "Low-key".

**For Techs like me**, that could be: "Access Point" or "AP", "dongle", "Firewall", "Knowledge Base", "phishing"

Some of these may look familiar, in a "i've seen all those words, just not in that order before." and they may also just be a jumble of letters "VGA", "HDMI", "USB-C". 

But all this begs the question; Why do we use it?!

A couple of reasons:

### Efficiency 
Jargon allows technicians to communicate complex ideas and technical details with fewer words.

### Professionalism
 Using specialized terminology signals expertise and helps technicians communicate effectively with colleagues and clients within their specific industry

### It's all we know
Frankly it can sometimes be the only thing that we know a certain app, device, or ruleset by. There isn't another name for a "VGA" cable, unless i call it a display cable, but that's not specific enough. That could mean so many other things!
- DisplayPort
- HDMI
- VGA
- DVI
- USB-C via Thunderbolt

### Great, so do I just have to learn all of it?
**Not at all!** If I ever start using jargon, because it happens sometimes, please ask me to explain. My job is to help you and train you. You don't have to know all the jargon to know how to fix something, or why something is acting the way that it is. There's **always** a simpler way to describe it.

All in all, Jargon is our professional way of talking to each other, and we get so good at the shortcut, that we forget how else to describe it. But **feel free to ask me** to simplify it, and i'll do my best!
