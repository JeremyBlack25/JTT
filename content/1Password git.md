Are your passwords safe? if someone got on your laptop, what could they log into? Your gradebook, your internet provider, phone provider, your bank account? Are your passwords written down in a notebook you keep readily available?

If any of those questions gave you a slight pause, then you probably need to learn about password security.
>"In 2023, individuals over the age of 60 accounted for the highest number of recorded cyber crime victims in the United States. According to the latest data, more than 104,068 people reported cyber crimes in the year examined. The second-most targeted were individuals between 30 and 39 years, with over 88 thousand complaints." [Link to Stats](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1390164/us-victims-cyber-crime-by-age/)

Cybercrimes can include everything from getting access to your gmail, stealing your data, or full identity theft.

One of the easiest things you can do to help protect yourself from most cybercrimes is using a password manager. As the name suggests, it keeps all your passwords locked in a "Vault" that only you have the "key" to. It makes it easier for you to log into all your websites that all have different passwords. You don't have to remember them, because it does that for you by filling in your username and password. You just have to provide your master password that acts as your "key".

The district has purchased and given all employees access to the #1 rated password manager, 1Password. It has loads of cool features, like telling if your password has been exposed in a data leak and if you need to change it. It also tells you the "strength" of your passwords and gives you tips to make a better password.

If you download your password manager on other devices, it gives you access to all your passwords everywhere! Phone, iPad, other computers, they all can have the app and make logging in much easier.
